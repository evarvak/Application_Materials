\section{Summary of my results}

My research concerns the structure of Lagrangian fibrations on symplectic varieties. 

\begin{definition} A normal variety \(X\) is {\bf symplectic} if it admits a non-degenerate 
        symplectic form \(\sigma \in H^0(X^\mathrm{sm}, \Omega_{X^\mathrm{sm}}^2)\) which 
        extends holomorphically onto a resolution of singularities \(\pi: Y \to X\).
        In this setting, a {\bf Lagrangian fibration} is a proper surjective morphism 
        \(f: X \to B\) with connected half-dimensional fibers, such that 
        \(\sigma|_{X_b} = 0\) for any fiber \(X_b\).
\end{definition}

We will need to assume that \(X\) is compact for most of our results. Additionally, 
we will want \(X\) to be ``irreducible'' in a sense, which will be made more precise 
in \S 2. To that end, the most general class of symplectic varieties we 
will work with is the primitive symplectic varieties. 

\begin{definition}
    A compact symplectic variety \(X\) is {\bf primitive symplectic} if it satisfies 
    \(H^1(X, \mathcal{O}_X) = 0\) and \(\sigma\) is unique up to scaling, i.e. 
    \(H^0(X, \Omega_X^2) = \C \sigma\).
\end{definition}

By Schwald \cite{schwald20}, when \(X\) is a primitive symplectic variety admitting 
a Lagrangian fibration \(f: X \to B\), the smooth fibers \(X_b\) are Abelian varieties. 
My main results further classify the structure of the fibers.

\begin{theorem}[citation needed*] \label{mt-classification}
    Let \(X\) be a primitive symplectic variety of dimension \(2n\) admitting a 
    Lagrangian fibration \(f: X \to B\). If \(f\) is not isotrivial, the Mumford--Tate 
    group of the very general fiber \(X_b\) is isogenous to \((\Sp_{2h})^{n/h}\).
\end{theorem}

Recall that an Abelian variety is Hodge-generic if it admits no extra Hodge tensors. 
In this case, it is simple and carries no extra symmetries.

\begin{corollary}[Citation needed*] \label{fiber-structure}
    The very general fiber \(X_b\) is isogenous to a product of Abelian varieties 
    \(A_1 \times \cdots \times A_{n/h}\), where each \(A_i\) is Hodge-generic and 
    \(A_i\) is not isogenous to \(A_j\) for \(i \neq j\).
\end{corollary}

The most well-studied class of primitive symplectic varieties consists of hyper-K\"ahler 
manifolds, which should be thought of as the natural generalization of K3 surfaces.

\begin{definition}
    A compact complex manifold \(X\) is {\bf hyper-K\"ahler} if \(X\) is simply connected 
    and satisfies \(H^0(X, \Omega_X^{2p}) = \C \sigma^p,\quad H^0(X, \Omega_X^{2p+1}) = 0.\)
\end{definition}

% In the case where \(X\) is a sufficiently general among hyper-K\"ahler manifolds 
% admitting a Lagrangian fibration, we can say much more about the Mumford--Tate group 
% of its very general fiber. 

\begin{theorem}[Citation needed*] \label{general-mt}
    Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be a deformation class of hyper-K\"ahler manifolds such that 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item all members \(X \in \mathcal{X}\) have second Betti number 
        \(b_2(X) \geq 7\),
        \item there exists some \(X \in \mathcal{X}\) admitting a Lagrangian fibration.
    \end{enumerate}
    Choose \(X\) to be general among the hyper-K\"ahler manifolds admitting a Lagrangian 
    fibration, \(f: X \to B\). If \(X_b\) is a very general fiber of \(f\), 
    then it has Mumford--Tate group \(\mathbf{MT}(X_b) = \mathrm{GSp}_{2n}\), i.e. 
    \(X_b\) is Hodge-generic.
\end{theorem}

These results largely came about through understanding the monodromy of the family \(f\). 
To be more precise, let \(f: X^\circ \to B^\circ\) be the restriction of \(f\) to 
its regular points, and let \(V = R^1 f_* \Z_{X^\circ}\), viewed as a local system. 

\begin{theorem}[\cite{varvak25}]
    Suppose that \(X\) is primitive symplectic, \(f: X \to B\) a Lagrangian fibration, and let
    \(V_\C = V \otimes_\Z \C\) the underlying \(\C\)-representation of 
    \(\pi_1(B^\circ)\). 
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item If \(f\) is not isotrivial, then \(V_\C\) is irreducible.
        \item If \(f\) is isotrivial, then its smooth fibers are isogenous to 
        \(E^n\), for \(E\) an elliptic curve. Furthermore, if \(K\) is the endomorphism 
        field of \(E\) (possibly \(\Q\)), then \(V_K = U_1 \oplus U_2\) splits into 
        two geometrically irreducible components.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark} \label{remark-monodromy}
There are a few clarifications to be made regarding the results and contributions. 
For the non-isotrivial case (1), the later result of \Cref{mt-classification} 
determines that a finite-index subgroup of the image of \(\pi_1(B^\circ)\) lies in 
\((\mathrm{Sp}_{2h, \Q})^{n/h}\), and acts on \(V_\C\) via a direct sum of standard representations. 
Such a representation would not be irreducible, if not for the unaccounted finite 
part of the monodromy transitively permuting the irreducible factors.
For the isotrivial case (2), the result \(X_b \sim E^n\) was first proven in 
\cite{kim-laza-martin24}, though we produced an alternative proof of this fact. 
\end{remark}




\section{Background and motivation}

Hyper-K\"ahler manifolds arise naturally in the study of K-trivial varieties. 
The following result is a classical theorem of Beauville--Bogomolov:
\begin{theorem}[Beauville--Bogomolov Decomposition Theorem]
    Let \(Z\) be a compact K\"ahler manifold with \(\omega_Z \simeq \mathcal{O}_Z\). 
    Then there is a finite \'etale cover \(\pi: Z' \to Z\) such that \(Z'\) has the form 
    \begin{equation*}
        Z' = T \times \prod_i X_i \times \prod_j Y_j,
    \end{equation*}
    where \(T\) is a complex torus, the \(X_i\) are hyper-K\"ahler manifolds, and 
    the \(Y_j\) are irreducible Calabi--Yau manifolds.
\end{theorem}

This theorem has since been generalized to allow for \(Z\) to have klt singularities 
(cf. \cite{bgl22}); in this case, the \(X_i\) are irreducible holomorphic 
symplectic varieties, a condition stronger than primitive symplectic but weaker than 
hyper-K\"ahler.

Thus the problem of understanding families of K-trivial varieties boils down to 
understanding families of complex tori, hyper-K\"ahlers, and ICYs. The former is 
largely classical, while the latter is also somewhat well-studied, largely due to work 
of Friedman--Laza. In contrast, the deformation spaces of hyper-K\"ahler manifolds 
are not as well understood.

Given a polarized hyper-K\"ahler manifold \((X, H)\), one can construct its deformation 
space as a type IV Shimura variety: take \(L = H^\perp \subsetneq H^2(X, \Z)\) with 
respect to the Beauville--Bogomolov--Fujiki form \(q\), and define 
\[\mathcal{D}_H = \{[\sigma] \in \bbP L_\C \mid q(\sigma, \sigma) = 0, \quad
q(\sigma, \ol\sigma) > 0\}.\]
Then the deformation space of \(X\) is constructed as an open subset 
\begin{equation*}
    \mathcal{X} \subseteq \leftQ{\mathcal{D}_H}{\Gamma} = 
    \leftQ{\frac{SO(2, b_2-3)}{SO(2) \times SO(b_2-3)}}{\Gamma},
\end{equation*}
where \(b_2 = \dim H^2(X, \C)\).

As such, to construct a deformation space of hyper-K\"ahler manifold, it suffices 
to exhibit one member. There are currently four known deformation types: the infinite 
families \(K3^{[n]}\) and \(\mathrm{Kum}_n\), occurring in every even dimension \(2n\),
and the two sporadic types \(OG6\) and \(OG10\), occurring in dimensions 6 and 10 
respectively. 

Now consider the following central conjecture.
\begin{conjecture}[Hyper-K\"ahler SYZ Conjecture] \label{conjecture-syz}
    Let \(\mathcal{X}\) be a deformation space of hyper-K\"ahler manifolds. There 
    exists some \(X \in \mathcal{X}\) which admits a Lagrangian fibration \(f: X \to B\).
\end{conjecture}

If true, one could make statements about all deformation classes of hyper-K\"ahlers 
by simply proving them for those admitting Lagrangian fibrations. A recent result 
\cite{engel25} assumed the SYZ conjecture to show that there are only finitely 
many deformation classes of hyper-K\"ahlers in any fixed dimension. 

As such, I believe it important to understand the structure of Lagrangian fibrations. 
Incidentally, it turns out that they carry many constraints.
\begin{theorem}
    Let \(X\) be a hyper-K\"ahler manifold of dimension \(2n\), \(f: X \to B\) a proper 
    surjective morphism with connected fibers, \(B \neq \{p\}\). The following all hold.
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \cite{greb-lehn14} \(f\) is a Lagrangian fibration.
        \item \cite{matsushita99} The smooth fibers of \(f\) are Abelian varieties. 
        \item \cite{matsushita16} \(B\) is \(\Q\)-Fano, \(\Q\)-factorial, and klt with 
        Picard rank 1. There is an isomorphism \(\IH^*(B, \Q) \simeq H^*(\bbP^n, \Q)\).
        \item \cite{hwang08} If \(B\) is smooth, then \(B \simeq \bbP^n\).
        \item \cite{bakker25} If \(f\) is not isotrivial, then it varies maximally, i.e. 
        the period map \(\cP: B^\circ \to \cA_{n}\) has an \(n\)-dimensional image.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

My results \Cref{fiber-structure}, \Cref{general-mt} aim to strengthen (2). Furthermore, 
as we will see in \S 3, this may yet have consequences for (3). The base \(B\) is a 
common area of study in this field, due to the following conjecture of Matsushita, 
partially resolved due by Hwang in (4).

\begin{conjecture}[Matsushita]
    Let \(X\) be a hyper-K\"ahler manifold, \(f: X \to B\) a Lagrangian fibration. 
    Then \(B \simeq \bbP^n\).
\end{conjecture}



\section{Future directions}

\subsection{A geometric characterization for the Mumford--Tate splitting}
Let \(f: X \to B\) be a Lagrangian fibration, and suppose the Mumford--Tate 
group of the very general fiber \(X_b\) is of the form 
\begin{equation} \label{eq-mt-factors}
    \mathbf{MT}(X_b) \sim (\Sp_{2h})^k, \quad k \neq 1.
\end{equation}
It is not hard to see that such a Lagrangian fibration exists. For example, we can take 
\(g: S \to \bbP^1\) to be an elliptically fibered K3 surface; the Hilbert scheme of points 
\(X := S^{[n]}\) is hyper-K\"ahler, and when \(g\) is not isotrivial, \(X\) admits 
a Lagrangian fibration 
\[f = g^{[n]}: X \to \bbP^n, \quad \mathbf{MT}(X_b) \sim (\SL_2)^n.\]

More generally, if we allow \(X\) to be primitive symplectic, it is easy to see that 
we can attain every positive integer pair \((h, k)\) in \eqref{eq-mt-factors}: 
by \Cref{general-mt}, we can find a Lagrangian fibration \(g: Y \to \bbP^h\) with 
\(\mathbf{MT}(Y_b) \sim \Sp_{2h}\), and taking the \(k\)-th symmetric power of 
this morphism produces 
\begin{equation*}
    X = Y^{(k)} \xrightarrow{g^{(k)}} (\bbP^h)^{(k)},
\end{equation*}
a Lagrangian fibration realizing the Mumford--Tate group \((\Sp_{2h})^k\).

However, notice that the base \((\bbP^h)^{(k)}\) is easily distinguished from 
projective space when \(h \neq 1\). This ensures that the argument above does not 
provide a {\it hyper-K\"ahler} manifold \(X\) realizing the prescribed Lagrangian 
fibration \(f: X \to B\).


\begin{question} \label{question-classification}
    Does there exist a hyper-K\"ahler manifold (or even an irreducible holomorphic 
    symplectic variety) \(X\) admitting a Lagrangian fibration \(f: X \to B\) 
    such that \(\mathbf{MT}(X_b) \sim (\Sp_{2h})^k\) for some \(h \neq 1, n\)?
\end{question}

I expect the answer to be negative. To motivate this somewhat, let 
\(V = R^1 f_* \Z_{X^\circ}\) be the local system underlying \(f\).
I claim there exists a ramified cover \(\pi: B' \to B\) which resolves the finite irreducible 
part of the monodromy, as referenced in \Cref{remark-monodromy}. To be more precise, 
\(\pi^* V_\Q = U_1 \oplus \cdots \oplus U_k\), and the monodromy action on 
each \(U_i\) factors through the standard representation of \(\Sp_{2h, \Q}\).

Now, using some methods from \cite{bakker-schnell23}, we can extend the isomorphism 
\(\cT_{B^\circ} \xrightarrow{\sim} F^1 \cV\) to the whole tangent bundle 
\(\cT_{B^\mathrm{sm}}\). By a local computation, we can pull this isomorphism back 
to get a splitting of the tangent bundle of \(B'\):
\begin{equation*}
    \cT_{{B'}^\mathrm{sm}} \simeq F^1 \ol{\mathcal{U}}_1 \oplus \cdots 
    \oplus F^1 \ol{\mathcal{U}}_k.
\end{equation*}

Another result of \cite{bakker-schnell23} shows that each \(F^1 \ol{\mathcal{U}}_i\) is closed 
under the Lie bracket, thus giving a foliation of \(B'\). By some more Hodge theory, 
we can actually show that the general leaf of this foliation is algebraic. 
Altogether, we can show that this produces the following diagram:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
X' \arrow{d}{f'} & X \arrow{d}{f} \\
B' \arrow{r}{\pi} \arrow[d, dashed, "\varphi^k"] & B \arrow[d, dashed, "\varphi^{(k)}"] \\
Z^k \arrow{r}{\ol{\pi}} & Z^{(k)}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}

Now we can try to answer \Cref{question-classification} by answering the following 
question: 
\begin{question}
    Does the map \(\varphi^{(k)}: B \dashrightarrow Z^{(k)}\) extend to a morphism?
\end{question}
If indeed it does, then we must have an inclusion on the level of intersection 
cohomology:
\begin{equation*}
    \IH^*(Z^{(k)}, \Q) = \mathrm{Sym}^k(\IH(Z, \Q)) \subseteq 
    \IH^*(B, \Q) = H^*(\bbP^n, \Q).
\end{equation*}

But \(H^*(\bbP^n, \Q)\) is minimal among \(n\)-dimensional K\"ahler manifolds, so 
we must have equality everywhere. This is impossible unless \(\dim Z = 1\), 
so that \(\IH^*(Z, \Q) = H^*(\bbP^1, \Q)\), which forces \(Z \simeq \bbP^1\). 
This immediately gives \(h = 1\) and \(k = n\), thereby providing a negative answer to
\Cref{question-classification}.

Another related question worth pursuing may also be seen in this diagram:
\begin{question} \label{question-birational-arrow}
    Is the map \(\varphi^{(k)}: B \dashrightarrow Z^{(k)}\) birational?
\end{question}
Suppose it were. By a theorem of Sacc\`a \cite{sacca25}, we can compactify the map 
\(\varphi^{(k)} \circ f: X \dashrightarrow Z^{(k)}\) to a morphism 
\(g: Y \to Z^{(k)}\), where \(Y\) is a smooth hyper-K\"ahler manifold birational 
to \(X\), and \(g: Y \to Z^{(k)}\) is a Lagrangian fibration agreeing with 
\(\varphi^{(k)} \circ f\) away from the indeterminacy locus.

Now by a similar intersection cohomology argument, we have
\(\IH^*(Z, \Q) = \IH^*(\bbP^1, \Q)\), forcing \(h = 1\) and \(k = n\), providing an 
negative answer to \Cref{question-classification}. However, in this setting, we 
may be able to learn more about \(X\). Over \(\bbP^1\), we may be able to take 
the variation of Hodge structures \(\mathcal{U}_i\) and produce a K3 surface 
\(S \to \bbP^1\) realizing the VHS. If so, we can show that \(Y \simeq S^{[n]}\), 
providing an affirmative answer to the following question:
\begin{question} \label{question-deformation-class}
    Let \(X\) be a hyper-K\"ahler manifold, \(f: X \to B\) a Lagrangian fibration. 
    Suppose \(\mathbf{MT}(X_b) \sim (\Sp_{2h})^k\), for \(h < n\). Does \(X\) belong 
    to the deformation type of \(K3^{[n]}\)?
\end{question}

It is possible that \Cref{question-birational-arrow} has a negative answer. In this case, 
it may still be possible to attack \Cref{question-deformation-class} in other ways. 
For instance, we may be able to show that the tangent bundle \(\cT_X\) splits 
in the same way the local system does. We can try to integrate the resulting distributions, 
which could give us a handle on how to construct \(X\) using lower-dimensional 
hyper-K\"ahler manifolds.



\subsection{Towards the hyper-K\"ahler Bogomolov--Mumford conjecture}

Recall the classical theorem of Bogomolov--Mumford: an ample linear series on a 
K3 surface contains a divisor supported on rational curves. An analogue of this 
theorem remains open for hyper-K\"ahler manifolds.

\begin{conjecture}[Hyper-K\"ahler Bogomolov--Mumford Conjecture] \label{conjecture-BM}
    Let \(X\) be a hyper-K\"ahler manifold, \(L\) an ample line bundle on \(X\). 
    Then there exists a uniruled divisor \(D \in |L|\).
\end{conjecture}

Currently, this conjecture is only known in full for the deformation types \(K3^{[n]}\) 
and \(\mathrm{Kum}_n\). Currently, I do not even have an idea on how to attack it 
in full generality, but \Cref{general-mt} may be useful in one aspect of this conjecture: 
producing an abundence of rational curves. 

If we start with \(X\) a general hyper-K\"ahler admitting a Lagrangian fibration 
\(f: X \to B\) as in the assumptions of \Cref{general-mt}, the image of the 
period map \(B^\circ \dashrightarrow \cA_n\) will be \(n\)-dimensional. On the other 
hand, the product locus \(\cA_1 \times \cA_{n-1} \subseteq \cA_n\) has 
codimension \(n-1\). The period image is Hodge-generic, so by Ax--Schanuel, it must 
meet \(\cA_1 \times \cA_{n-1}\) in the expected dimension \(n - (n-1) = 1\). 

In other words, there is a curve \(C^\circ \subseteq B^\circ\) over which the 
family of Abelian varieties \(X_{C^\circ} \to C^\circ\) is isogenous to 
a product family. It should be reasonable to show that over the boundary of \(C^\circ\), 
the elliptic part of the family degenerates into a rational curve, while the 
non-elliptic part remains non-degenerate. This gives rise to a uniruled algebraic 
cycle of dimension \(n+1\) in \(\mathrm{CH}^*(X)\).

Thus, we have methods of producing rational curves in the singular fibers of \(f\). 
However, we want to produce rational curves on all hyper-K\"ahler manifolds, not simply 
the ones admitting Lagrangian fibrations. If we assume \Cref{conjecture-syz}, 
this reduces to the following question:
\begin{question}
    Let \(X\) be a hyper-K\"ahler manifold admitting a Lagrangian fibration \(f\). 
    Let \(Z \subseteq X\) be a singular rational curve contained in a singular fiber 
    of \(f\), obtained as above. Let \(X'\) be a general deformation of \(X\); 
    does \(Z\) deform to a rational curve \(Z' \subseteq X'\)?
\end{question}

Finally, if we truly hope to attack \Cref{conjecture-BM}, we would want to break 
a divisor \(D \in |L|\) into the union \(D \sim D^\mathrm{vert} + D^\mathrm{base}\), 
where \(D^\mathrm{vert}\) is a uniruled divisor which is vertical with respect to the Lagrangian 
fibration, and \(D^\mathrm{base}\) is a uniruled divisor which is pulled back from 
the base. One possible approach may be to deform away from a Lagrangian fibration 
\(f: X \to B\) which admits a rational section \(s: B \dashrightarrow X\).

\begin{question}
    Can we find a special uniruled decomposition 
    \(D \sim D^\mathrm{vert} + D^\mathrm{base}\) which deforms to a unirational divisor 
    under general deformations?
\end{question}
